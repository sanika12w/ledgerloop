## Interview 1 — [Name / Initials]
**Role:** Head of Engineering  
**Company Stage:** Seed-stage SaaS (15–25 employees)

### Key Quotes
- "We literally have no idea how much we spend on AI tools until Stripe hits us."
- "Half the team uses Cursor, the other half uses Copilot — I don’t even know why both exist."
- "We don’t track AI spend like we track cloud costs, it’s just messy."

### Most surprising insight
They didn’t care about optimization — they cared about **visibility first**.

### What this changed in my design
I realized the product should show *spend clarity first*, not savings recommendations.

## Interview 2 — [Name / Initials]
**Role:** Founder  
**Company Stage:** Early Series A (30–50 employees)

### Key Quotes
- "We thought AI tools were cheap until we hit $1.2K/month."
- "Nobody owns AI tool spending internally."
- "I don’t trust spreadsheets for this anymore."

### Most surprising insight
They already *suspected waste*, but had no system to confirm it.

### What this changed in my design
I made audit output more visual and less spreadsheet-like.

## Interview 3 — [Name / Initials]
**Role:** Senior Engineer  
**Company Stage:** Bootstrapped SaaS

### Key Quotes
- "We just keep adding tools without removing old ones."
- "I think we’re paying for 3 overlapping AI writing tools."
- "If someone showed me where to cut cost, I’d act immediately."

### Most surprising insight
They are actively waiting for someone to tell them what to remove.

### What this changed in my design
I prioritized “remove suggestions” over “add recommendations.”
